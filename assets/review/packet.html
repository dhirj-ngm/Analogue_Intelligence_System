Instructions and label meanings

Cases are shuffled. You are not shown search scores, result positions or expected answers. Some descriptions may have no useful comparison. Judge each case on its evidence and the situation's dates; a shared word alone is insufficient.

  • Useful: helps analyse the stated situation, with explainable differences.
  • Partly useful: covers a limited aspect or offers an important contrast.
  • Not useful: does not support the comparison. Explain the mismatch.
  • Unsure: you cannot decide from the available evidence. Explain what is missing.

Separately judge whether presenting a case as a comparison would mislead a reader. A useful comparison does not prove an earlier action will work now. Do not consult project evaluation labels or use AI to supply judgements. Note whether you opened an original report; source checking is encouraged, and inaccessible sources should be recorded.

Judge only information available by the stated date. Figures may cover different parts of a business or use different calculations. For margins, 100 basis points means one percentage point. A price benchmark is not necessarily the company's actual purchase price.
