## Episode continuity: task guidance, not a finished policy

Use time distance to flag possible recurrence, then inspect company/business
scope, reported pressure, affected metrics, source references to continuing
conditions, responses and any evidence of resolution or a distinct new shock.
Same words and nearby dates alone are insufficient. A long reporting gap can
also be missing coverage; it does not establish that the challenge ended.

For the example of four continuing quarterly disclosures, assign one episode
ID and four linked document/update records. Preserve update-specific dates,
claims, actions and contradictions. If a later recurrence is split into another
episode, retain a relationship and a written reason. Ambiguous cases get a
review-needed note, not a fabricated boundary. No numeric gap is chosen here.

Clarification has two owners: the reader supplies their situation and objective;
curation/source review resolves historical case identity. Asking the reader
cannot establish undocumented facts about a historical company.

## Product conversation and pilot are different layers

The eventual product is conversational: understand the question, ask only useful
clarifications, retrieve reviewed evidence, explain a case, then explore supported
details requested by the reader. Adapt presentation, not evidence or truthfulness.
No mandatory LLM/chatbot implementation is added to M001; fixed query records
can test the retrieval foundation first. “Pilot” refers to the feasibility study;
the person conversing with the eventual application is the reader/user.

Manager proposes retrospective case-study learning as the initial product
experience because the user prioritizes understanding cases and outcomes. This
is a proposed default, not a user-selected date policy. Before T05, choose and
record the evaluation mode, historical ordering and cutoff rules explicitly.
Existing follow-up/ranking separation remains in force.
